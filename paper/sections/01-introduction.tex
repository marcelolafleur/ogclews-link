\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% motivates the link and positions it in the literature; THIS paper is the formal
% development and validation — the current state of the integration.

How should a bottom-up model of a country's energy system be joined to a
general-equilibrium model of its economy so that each number crossing the boundary
has a stated economic meaning, enters exactly once, and can be audited? The question
is practical. Least-cost planning frameworks in the \CLEWS{} tradition
\citep{howells_clews_2013,howells_osemosys_2011} decide what a national energy
transition builds and runs, but they contain no households, firms, or government,
and no price that any agent responds to. Overlapping-generations (OLG)
general-equilibrium platforms such as \OGCore{} \citep{debacker_evans_ogcore} carry
behaviour, fiscal closure, and the distribution of welfare across ages and incomes,
but no energy technology---in the shipped model, energy is not even a priced input
to production. Neither family alone can say what a transition plan does to output,
prices, the public budget, or the households that bear it. A companion paper
\citep{lafleur_linkages} develops that motivation and positions the work within the
energy--macro integration literature; this paper is the formal side. It develops the
coupling as an explicit framework and reports the validation of each mechanism, so
that the current state of the integration---what is built, what is verified, and
what remains open---can be assessed, and criticised, at the level of its parts.

The framework reduces the coupling to two primitives and a discipline. A
\emph{signal} is a quantity read from a solved \CLEWS{} scenario: the electricity
price, the capital a build-out requires, an emissions path. A \emph{wedge} is the
\OGCore{} parameter that expresses it: a consumption-tax vector, an industry
productivity level, the public-investment share, an age-specific mortality or
effective-labour profile. A \emph{channel} is a named, guard-railed map from a
signal to a deliberately partitioned set of wedges that never overlap---in the
simplest channels, exactly one. Eight channels realise the coupling: four carry \CLEWS{}
signals into \OGCore{} (the energy price, public grid investment, generation
capital intensity, and air-quality health), two are policy instruments applied once
to both models (a carbon price and an investment tax credit), and two read
\OGCore{} outputs back to \CLEWS{} (the equilibrium return and sectoral activity).
Around the channels sits an accounting discipline---stated as principles, enforced
by guards in the code and by the rules of experiment composition---whose single
purpose is that no economic quantity is counted twice. The machinery is country-agnostic: no country, industry index, or
industry count is hard-coded, and the same channels re-point at another country
through a declared interface contract.

The link is soft---the models remain separate programs exchanging a small set of
auditable files---and this is a conclusion rather than a convenience. \OGCore{}
traces an OLG transition path period by period; \OSeMOSYS{} solves one
perfect-foresight linear program over all years at once. Hard links in the
\textsc{message-macro} tradition
\citep{messner_schrattenholzer_2000,manne_merge_1995,loulou_times_2008} fold the
macro side into the energy optimisation through a scalar welfare objective, and an
OLG economy---an equilibrium among overlapping cohorts, not a planner's
optimum---has none to offer. What replaces the joint program is a staged
orchestration: solve the \OGCore{} baseline; solve the \CLEWS{} scenario and
extract its signals; apply the wedges and solve the reform transition; emit the
economy's return and activity back to \CLEWS{}; and, in the closed loop, re-solve
\CLEWS{} and iterate to a fixed point in the energy price and quantity.

Two design choices carry most of the economics. The first is the price signal. The
theoretically clean object is the shadow price on the \OSeMOSYS{} commodity-balance
constraint---the marginal cost of one more unit of electricity, and under the
competitive interpretation the market-clearing price. In practice that shadow price
is degenerate: the constraint binds in scattered years and the price is near zero
elsewhere. The deployed signal is therefore the levelized cost of electricity---the
all-in average cost of a delivered unit---a dense, robust proxy, with the shadow
price reserved for scarcity analyses. Whatever its source, the price plays two
roles the energy model cannot: it makes energy demand price-responsive (demand is
fixed inside the optimiser), and it reveals incidence, because \OGCore{}'s
households, indexed by age and lifetime income, spend different shares of their
budgets on energy. The second choice is how an electricity price enters an economy
whose production function has no energy input. The energy channel splits
electricity by use: the share households consume directly enters as a
consumption-price wedge, and the share industries buy as an input enters as an
input--output-calibrated cost-push on industry productivities---roughly one quarter
and three quarters for the Philippines---with electricity's own self-use netted out
so the two sides never overlap. Neither side alone is the channel: the wedge is
close to output-neutral, the cost-push is contractionary, and only the composite
carries the price into both the cost of living and the cost of production. The
structural destination---energy as a priced input in the production
technology---is specified but out of scope here.

The discipline earns its place where intuition mislabels mechanisms. A capex-heavy
clean power system invites the reading ``capital-intensive, so it pulls capital
in,'' and the natural lever is the energy industry's capital exponent. The model's
own first-order condition says otherwise: raising the exponent $12.2\%$ collapses
the electricity price $42.8\%$ and \emph{sheds} $22.4\%$ of the sector's
capital---a response the firm identity $\hat K_E=\hat\gamma_E\,\hat p_E\,\hat Y_E$
reproduces to within $0.03\%$---while the instrument that does draw capital in, an
investment tax credit, operates on the cost of capital, where the exponent is
absent. The framework treats the two as distinct channels and forbids stacking
them. The health channel, finally, is the mechanism the OLG structure makes
possible: a power-sector emissions path maps, through a calibrated dose-response
and the Global Burden of Disease age profiles, onto age-specific mortality and
effective labour---so cleaner air changes who is alive, at which ages, and with
what productivity, inside general equilibrium rather than as an external valuation
appended to an energy model's output.

We validate the machinery end-to-end on the Philippines: the country's \CLEWS{}
case---a baseline against an announced energy-policy reform---coupled to a
multi-industry \OGCore{} calibration, with each experiment's macroeconomic
aggregates regression-locked in a committed record (sectoral and interface
quantities are reproduced from the solved outputs and qualified where they appear). The reform raises the levelized cost of electricity about
$5\%$ on average, peaking near $16\%$ around 2030. Four results characterise the
coupled response. The macroeconomic effect changes sign with the horizon: output is
about $+0.2\%$ along the first decade of the transition---the working-age morbidity
gain from cleaner air---but $-0.14\%$ in the long-run steady state, where higher
energy costs combine with a survival gain concentrated among the elderly. The
coupled steady state is nearly an order of magnitude larger than the sum of the
standalone channel effects ($-0.138\%$ against roughly $-0.02\%$): the channels
interact in general equilibrium, and no additive reading of per-channel results
reproduces the joint run. Incidence is regressive even under full revenue
recycling: aggregate consumption falls about $0.09\%$, with the burden concentrated
on lower-income households, for whom electricity is closer to a necessity. And the
health effect is disciplined by its dose-response: the reform's $2.7\%$ cut in
power-sector particulate emissions saves about $95$ lives per year, not the
$1{,}165$ that a one-for-one scaling of the attributable burden would claim.

Because this paper doubles as a status report on the integration, we state the
frontier precisely. Implemented and validated: the full \CLEWS{}$\to$\OGCore{} pass---the channel
library exercised by a continuable battery and locked in the golden record, all but
the investment-tax-credit lever, which is defined but not yet run standalone
(\Cref{sec:channels})---and the \CLEWS{} re-solve seam that loop closure requires, in which the
link patches a copy of the energy case, re-solves it in the energy model's own
environment, and reads the result; a controlled $+10\%$ household-electricity
demand perturbation returns a $+4.3\%$ electricity-production response. Designed
but not yet wired: the outer controller that iterates the re-solve to the
price--quantity fixed point under damping; until it runs, the two
\OGCore{}$\to$\CLEWS{} channels emit well-formed signals with no feedback effect on
the macro economy. Open by construction: the bridge between \CLEWS{} monetary units
and the \OGCore{} numéraire is currently set to unity, so the carbon and investment
magnitudes are labelled illustrative wherever they appear; the energy-price ratio
and the health inputs do not depend on it.
