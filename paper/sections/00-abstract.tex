Bottom-up resource-planning models decide what an energy transition builds; they
contain no households, firms, or government. Overlapping-generations (OLG)
general-equilibrium models carry behaviour, fiscal closure, and distribution; they
contain no energy technology. This paper explains how the two are joined by an
auditable soft link: \CLEWS{}/\OSeMOSYS{} on the resource side, and on the economy
side \OGCore{}, an overlapping-generations model with forward-looking cohorts,
income heterogeneity, demography, and
fiscal closure. Eight channels carry electricity costs, public investment, carbon
policy, pollution health effects, the equilibrium return, and activity across the
boundary; each names its source quantity, its economic interpretation, its
destination parameter, and the accounting rule that keeps any cost from entering
twice. Because the commodity-balance shadow price is degenerate in the application,
the deployed price signal is the levelized cost of electricity, split between a
household consumption wedge and an input--output-calibrated industry cost-push. A
Philippine application validates the mechanisms jointly: near-term output gains
from working-age health, a small long-run contraction from electricity costs and
demographic composition, and regressive incidence even under full revenue
recycling. The single pass and the \CLEWS{} re-solve seam are implemented and
tested; the outer fixed-point iteration and the monetary-unit bridge remain open.
