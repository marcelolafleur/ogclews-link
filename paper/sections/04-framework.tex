\section{The Coupling Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The framework reduces the coupling to two primitive objects and a discipline for
combining them. A \emph{signal} is a quantity carried out of a solved \CLEWS{}
scenario; a \emph{wedge} is a parameter of \OGCore{} that a signal sets; a
\emph{channel} is a named, guard-railed map from a signal to a set of wedges
partitioned so that no part of the signal enters twice---one wedge in the simplest
channels, several in those that split a signal by use (the energy channel,
\Cref{sec:routes}) or by demographic object (health, \Cref{sec:ch-health}). The remainder of
this section defines these objects (\Cref{sec:signals-wedges}), identifies the
privileged signal---the marginal cost on the energy-balance constraint
(\Cref{sec:signal})---shows how the energy channel carries an electricity-price change into
a core that contains no energy in production (\Cref{sec:routes}), describes the staged
orchestration and the interface contract that make the mapping auditable
(\Cref{sec:architecture}), and states the accounting principles that keep a single
signal from being counted twice (\Cref{sec:discipline}).

\subsection{Signals and wedges}
\label{sec:signals-wedges}

The two models meet across an asymmetry. \OGCore{} is rich in behaviour, public
finance, and household heterogeneity but blind to energy technology; \CLEWS{} is rich
in technology, dispatch, and emissions but blind to behaviour, the fiscal sector, and
distribution. The information that usefully crosses the seam therefore differs by
direction: from the macro side flow \emph{quantities}---sectoral activity and final
demand; from the energy side flow \emph{prices and physical outcomes}---marginal
costs, capital requirements, and emissions. The two primitives formalise the two
directions.

\begin{definition}[Signal]
A \emph{signal} is a scalar or time path extracted from a solved \CLEWS{} scenario:
the marginal cost on a commodity-balance constraint, the capital investment
required by a build-out, the emissions of a fuel or period, or the marginal cost on an
emissions constraint (an implied carbon price).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Wedge]
A \emph{wedge} is a parameter of \OGCore{} whose perturbation expresses a signal as a
change in household or firm behaviour or in the government budget: the
consumption-tax vector $\tauc$, an industry total-factor-productivity level $\TFP_m$,
the public-investment share $\alphaI$, or the demographic objects---the age-specific
mortality rate $\rho(s,t)$ and effective-labour profile $e(t,s,j)$.
\end{definition}

The term is used in its public-finance sense: the simplest channel
(\Cref{sec:routes}) literally inserts a tax wedge between the producer and consumer
price of energy. A channel is then the composition of an extractor and an applicator,
together with the validation that fires when a mapping is large or ill-posed.

\begin{remark}[Realisation]
The channels are plain functions in \texttt{channels.py} (those that run
\OGCore{}$\to$\CLEWS{} carry an \texttt{emit\_} prefix and execute after the reform
solves); extraction lives in \texttt{signals.py}, application in \texttt{og\_wedge.py},
the per-run interface and sector concordance in \texttt{contract.py}/\texttt{discovery.py},
orchestration in \texttt{framework.py}, and the cross-environment solve---which runs the
country's \OGCore{} model in its own interpreter and exchanges data files---in
\texttt{registry.py}/\texttt{models.py}/\texttt{og\_runner.py}. Everything is
country-agnostic: no hard-coded country, industry index, or industry count are baked in;
those are read from the calibration and the declared concordance (\Cref{sec:architecture}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The levelized cost of electricity as the load-bearing signal}
\label{sec:signal}

Among the signals, the price of electricity is privileged. Its theoretically clean form
is a marginal cost --- the \emph{shadow price} on the \OSeMOSYS{} commodity-balance
constraint \citep{howells_osemosys_2011} (which requires supply to meet demand in every
timeslice and year), what the least-cost system would pay to deliver one more unit, and
under the competitive interpretation the price that clears the market. But that shadow
price is degenerate in this model: it binds in only scattered years and is near-zero
elsewhere, so it is unusable as a smooth long-run signal (it is kept for scarcity
studies). The coupling therefore uses the \emph{levelized cost of electricity} (LCOE):
the all-in average cost of a unit of delivered power --- capital recovery, operation and
maintenance, and fuel --- a dense, robust proxy for the price the economy faces. Two
properties make it load-bearing.

First, it closes an elasticity gap. Inside the energy optimiser, demand is an
exogenous datum.

\begin{assumption}[Price-inelastic energy demand in \CLEWS{}]
\label{ass:inelastic}
\CLEWS{}/\OSeMOSYS{} solves for the least-cost system that meets a \emph{fixed} energy-
service demand; quantity demanded does not respond to the commodity price within the
optimiser.
\end{assumption}

\noindent The behavioural response must therefore come from the macro side. Feeding
this price to \OGCore{} households---whose multi-good demand is unit-elastic above a
Stone--Geary subsistence floor \citep{stone_1954}, see \Cref{sec:og}---makes the
quantity of energy demanded respond to its price, supplying the elasticity the
optimiser lacks.

Second, it reveals incidence. A single program-level ``cost'' is silent on who bears
it. \OGCore{}'s households, indexed by $\Ngen$ age and $\Jinc$ lifetime-income groups,
distribute the burden of an energy-price change; because energy is a partial
necessity, the burden is generally regressive---an object a single-agent core cannot
produce.

These two roles define what it means for the coupling to be internally consistent.

\begin{definition}[Soft-link equilibrium]
\label{def:fixedpoint}
Let $D_{\text{OG}}(\pi)$ be the energy demand \OGCore{} implies when households (and,
in the structural form of \Cref{sec:routes}, firms) face energy price $\pi$, and let
$\pi_{\text{C}}(D)$ be the \CLEWS{} price of delivering $D$ (its levelized cost). A \emph{consistent} coupling is a pair $(\pi^\star, D^\star)$ with
\[
  D^\star = D_{\text{OG}}(\pi^\star)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \pi^\star = \pi_{\text{C}}(D^\star).
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent A single \CLEWS{}$\to$\OGCore{} pass evaluates $\pi_{\text{C}}(D^{b})$ at
\emph{baseline} demand and applies it once; iterating the two maps to the fixed point
of \Cref{def:fixedpoint} is \emph{loop closure} (\Cref{sec:architecture}). With the
levelized cost as the deployed price, the fixed point is consistency under that
pricing proxy, not a competitive-equilibrium price---the distinction inherits from
the signal choice above. The
present work validates the single pass and the \CLEWS{} re-solve seam that the closed
loop requires; the principal unbuilt piece is the outer iteration controller.

\begin{remark}[Shadow-price hygiene]
\label{rem:hygiene}
That the theoretically privileged signal fails in practice is a general lesson, and
we convert it into a protocol applied to any marginal cost read from the optimiser
before it crosses the seam: the value must sit above the solver's own reporting
resolution; it must be invariant across alternate optima---a price that moves while
nothing physical changes is degeneracy, not information; it must be decomposed
against any cost parameter that floors it; and it must trace to a sourced input.
The electricity marginal cost fails the first two tests, which is the origin of the
levelized-cost choice above. Preliminary work applying the same protocol to the
land balance of the Philippine case finds the same failure modes there, so we treat
the protocol as part of the framework rather than a one-off diagnosis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The energy channel: who pays when electricity gets dearer}
\label{sec:routes}

The design of this channel follows from asking one question concretely: when the
reform makes a delivered unit of electricity about $5\%$ more expensive, \emph{who
pays}? In the Philippine social accounting matrix the answer has two parts: industries,
which buy roughly three quarters of electricity as a production input, and households,
which consume the remaining quarter directly. A faithful transmission must reach both
groups of buyers, and must charge each peso of the increase exactly once.

The difficulty is that only one of those buyer groups exists in \OGCore{}. Two
structural facts, stated exactly. First, production is value added only: each industry
produces with private capital, public capital, and labour (\Cref{sec:og}), and
\emph{no firm buys anything from any other firm}---there is no line in the
manufacturing sector's cost function where electricity appears, at any price. Second,
the model's input--output object is a \emph{consumption} bridge, not an
intermediate-use matrix: a fixed $\Igood\times\Mind$ map that converts industry
outputs into consumption goods, $p_i=\sum_m \omega_{im}p_m$, with no substitution
across industries. Electricity's price can therefore reach households through the
goods bridge, but the three quarters of the increase that industrial buyers should
pay has no structural path---the buyers are missing from the model. Everything in
this subsection is the consequence of that fact.

\paragraph{The firm-side route, and why it delivers only a quarter of the shock.}
The natural first instinct is to represent the change at the electricity
\emph{producer}: let the industry price $p_{m_e}$ itself rise, and let households
respond to that. \OGCore{} has no parameter that inserts a wedge between what a
firm's output sells for and what the firm receives---prices are outcomes, not
dials---but it has an exact equivalent handle. Because every industry prices at unit
cost (zero profit), $p_m$ is proportional to $1/\TFP_m$ holding factor prices, so
dividing the electricity industry's productivity by the price ratio,
$\TFP_{m_e}\to\TFP_{m_e}/g_t$, makes the model \emph{produce} the higher electricity
price endogenously---with the full general-equilibrium response of wages, the
interest rate, and the sector's own inputs that a tax wedge does not have. This route
is implemented (\texttt{energy\_price\_tfp}). Now trace where the higher price goes.
It flows through the consumption bridge into the energy good, and households pay it.
It goes nowhere else: manufacturing's costs, prices, and decisions are untouched,
because manufacturing buys no electricity in the model. The route is not wrong---it is
\emph{incomplete by construction}: it can deliver the shock only to the quarter of its
real-world destination that the model represents. It also has a subtler liability: it
encodes a \emph{system}-cost change as an electricity-\emph{technology} change, so the
sector's own capital and labour reorganise in response to a productivity event that
did not occur in the scenario.

\paragraph{The household quarter: a consumption wedge, made fiscally inert.}
The deployed channel therefore carries the household share through the more surgical
instrument: a consumption-tax wedge on the energy good $i_e$, set so the gross-of-tax
price inherits the ratio,
\begin{equation}
  1 + \tau^{c,r}_{t,i_e} \;=\; g_t\,\bigl(1 + \tau^{c,b}_{t,i_e}\bigr),
  \label{eq:routeA}
\end{equation}
where $g_t \equiv \pi^{r}_t/\pi^{b}_t$ is the \CLEWS{} price ratio. Households then
face $(1+\tauc_{t,i_e})\,p_{i_e,t}$ exactly where \OGCore{} puts consumption taxes:
in the cost-of-living index, in subsistence spending, and in the sub-good demand.
Demand falls, and---because energy is a partial necessity---the burden is regressive,
the channel's distinctive incidence output. The wedge raises what households pay
without fabricating a technology event inside the electricity sector; its cost is
fiscal bookkeeping. A consumption tax mechanically credits the government with
revenue, but an electricity-price increase is not a tax: it is a real resource cost,
and no government receives it. Left alone, the wedge would therefore smuggle an
accidental fiscal consolidation into the run---extra revenue retiring debt under the
closure rule. The channel neutralises this by returning the wedge's revenue to
households as a lump-sum transfer (a bump to the transfer share $\alphaT$, estimated
on baseline quantities): the wedge is \emph{revenue-neutral by construction},
delivering relative prices and incidence and nothing else. This side alone is close
to output-neutral: it reshuffles consumption rather than shrinking output
(\Cref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{The industrial three quarters: delivered by hand.}
For the share the missing buyers should pay, the channel does the only thing the
structure allows: it raises each industry's costs directly, by the amount its
electricity purchases imply. Let
$\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_{\Mind})$ be the direct electricity cost
shares and $A$ the matrix of interindustry input coefficients, both read from the
social accounting matrix. A proportional price increase $\hat p_E$ raises industry
$m$'s unit cost directly by $\theta_m\hat p_E$ and, through purchased intermediates,
indirectly; summing the Leontief series gives the total cost-push
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf c} \;=\; (I - A^{\!\top})^{-1}\,\boldsymbol\theta\,\hat p_E ,
  \label{eq:routeB}
\end{equation}
applied as a productivity haircut $\TFP^{r}_m = \TFP^{b}_m\,(1-\kappa_m)$, with
$\kappa_m$ the gross-output cost increase of industry $m$ and electricity's own
self-use netted out so that nothing appears in both legs. The \emph{size} of the
shock comes from data; the \emph{mechanism} is imposed, because the structural door
does not exist. That is the honest characterisation of this leg: it is the
first-order cost pass-through an intermediate-input structure would deliver, without
the structure---firms do not substitute away from electricity, and no energy market
clears. It is the contractionary, wage-bearing side, with the correct output sign
(\Cref{sec:results}).

\begin{remark}[The channel is the composite, with a division of labour]
\label{rem:ab-contrast}
The deployed energy channel applies both legs at once, weighted by electricity's
final-versus-intermediate split in the country's social accounting matrix. The
division of labour is deliberate: the \emph{wedge} carries relative prices and
incidence, fiscally neutralised by the rebate; the \emph{cost-push} carries the real
resource loss, as destroyed productive capacity. Neither alone is the channel---the
wedge is close to output-neutral, the cost-push materially contractionary
(\Cref{sec:results})---and each peso of the price increase enters exactly once:
intermediate exposure through $\boldsymbol\theta$, final exposure through the wedge,
the overlap (self-use) removed.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The structural ideal: put the missing buyers into the model.}
The fix for all of the above is structural: give firms the electricity purchases they
are missing, by nesting energy with value added in the technology,
\begin{equation}
  Y^{\text{gross}}_m
  \;=\; \TFP_m\Bigl[\,\omega_m\, VA_m^{\frac{\sigma_{E,m}-1}{\sigma_{E,m}}}
        + (1-\omega_m)\, E_m^{\frac{\sigma_{E,m}-1}{\sigma_{E,m}}}\Bigr]
        ^{\frac{\sigma_{E,m}}{\sigma_{E,m}-1}},
  \label{eq:routeC}
\end{equation}
where $VA_m$ is the existing capital--labour aggregate and $E_m$ is energy purchased
from the energy industry $m_e$ at the price $p_{m_e}$. Cost minimisation yields
the energy demand
\begin{equation}
  E_m \;=\; \theta_m\Bigl(\frac{p_{m_e}}{MC_m}\Bigr)^{-\sigma_{E,m}} Y^{\text{gross}}_m ,
  \label{eq:routeC-foc}
\end{equation}
the energy industry clears a resource constraint
$Y_{m_e} = (\text{final demand}) + \sum_{m\ne m_e} E_m$, output is measured as value
added net of energy purchases, and the energy price feeds firm cost endogenously so
that the fixed point of \Cref{def:fixedpoint} closes the loop on quantities as well as
prices. The substitution elasticities $\sigma_{E,m}\in[0.3,0.8]$ are available from the
energy-CGE literature and the cost shares $\theta_m$ are already calibrated from the
Philippine social accounting matrix (\Cref{sec:calibration}); indeed the SAM already
contains the full intermediate-use block, so the barrier is \OGCore{}'s firm side,
not data. With it, the two-legged composite collapses into one mechanism---firms
substitute away from expensive electricity, an energy market clears, and the fixed
point of \Cref{def:fixedpoint} closes on quantities as well as prices. It is a
production-side extension to \OGCore{} itself, out of scope here.

\subsection{Architecture: a staged soft link}
\label{sec:architecture}

The classic hard-link route is ruled out on structural, not practical, grounds.

\begin{assumption}[Incompatible solution concepts]
\label{ass:hardlink}
\OGCore{} solves an overlapping-generations transition path period by period under
foresight; \OSeMOSYS{} is a single perfect-foresight linear program over all years at
once. \OGCore{} exposes no scalar welfare into which the program could be folded, and
no shock can be injected mid-solve.
\end{assumption}

\noindent The assumption forecloses the integrated-welfare-program route of the
hard-link tradition; a different simultaneous formulation---stacking both models'
equilibrium and optimality conditions as one complementarity system---is
conceivable in principle but would be a research programme of its own, not an
available route.

\noindent The models therefore stay separate and exchange a few signals---a soft link,
in the tradition of iteratively coupled energy--macro models
\citep{messner_schrattenholzer_2000,manne_merge_1995,loulou_times_2008}, but with a
behaviourally and fiscally rich overlapping-generations core in place of a
representative agent. The orchestration (\Cref{fig:architecture}) runs in stages:
(i)~solve the \OGCore{} baseline in steady state and along the transition;
(ii)~solve the \CLEWS{} scenario and extract its signals; (iii)~apply the channel
wedges to the reform parameters and solve the reform transition; (iv)~read the
\OGCore{}$\to$\CLEWS{} signals (the equilibrium return $\rp$ and sectoral activity);
and, in the closed loop, (v)~re-solve \CLEWS{} and repeat to the fixed point of
\Cref{def:fixedpoint}.

\diagramfig{architecture}{The structural seam. The two models stay separate and pass
signals across a contract; a hard link is precluded by \Cref{ass:hardlink}.}{fig:architecture}

What makes the exchange auditable is an explicit interface contract
(\texttt{contract.py}): a \emph{scenario pair} naming the baseline and reform runs, the
\CLEWS{} scenario, and the country; a \emph{concordance} mapping \OGCore{} industries
$m$ and goods $i$ to \CLEWS{} commodities, technologies, and demands---supplied by the
country configuration, since \OGCore{} has no native notion of a resource; and a
\emph{unit map} (real versus nominal, base year, deflator) that bridges \CLEWS{}'s bare
numbers to \OGCore{}'s real numéraire. The contract is what lets the same machinery be
re-pointed at another country without touching the channels.

Loop closure is constrained by \Cref{ass:inelastic}. Because energy demand is
price-inelastic inside the optimiser, naïve price feedback can oscillate; convergence
to \Cref{def:fixedpoint} requires damping and a baseline-anchored demand calibration.
The \OGCore{}$\to$\CLEWS{} emitters produce well-formed \CLEWS{} inputs, and the
\CLEWS{} re-solve seam they are designed to feed---step~(v) of the orchestration---is
implemented and validated with a controlled perturbation rather than planned: the link
patches a copy of the \CLEWS{} case, re-solves it
in the energy model's own environment, and reads the result, leaving the calibrated case
untouched. On the Philippine case a controlled $+10\%$ household-electricity demand
patch propagates through the re-solve to a $+4.3\%$ electricity-production response,
confirming the seam transmits; feeding the \emph{emitted} signals through it end to end
belongs to the outer controller. What is not yet wired is the
outer controller that iterates the re-solve to \Cref{def:fixedpoint} under damping; until
it runs, the two \OGCore{}$\to$\CLEWS{} channels (\Cref{sec:ch-discount,sec:ch-demand})
emit well-formed signals but have no feedback effect on the macro economy.

\subsection{No-double-counting discipline}
\label{sec:discipline}

Because several signals can be expressed through more than one wedge---and because a
fiscal instrument used as a price wedge also moves the budget---the framework needs
explicit accounting rules. We state them as principles; each is enforced by a guard in
the corresponding channel where the forbidden combination can arise within one channel,
and by the composition rules of the experiment layer where it arises across channels.

\begin{principle}[One carbon price]
A carbon price enters the system once: as an emissions penalty in \CLEWS{} and as a
tax wedge in \OGCore{} along the same path. It is never \emph{also} inferred a second
time from a \CLEWS{} shadow price.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Count each energy cost once]
Represent each unit of an energy cost once. The energy channel (\Cref{sec:routes})
partitions electricity by use---a consumption wedge on the household share, a cost-push
on the intermediate share, with electricity's own self-use netted out---so the two sides
do not overlap. What is forbidden is a second representation of the \emph{same} flow: a
$\tauc$ wedge and a $\TFP$ haircut on the same consumption, or a separately-inferred
carbon wedge stacked on top.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[One view of generation capital]
The capital cost of a generation build-out is represented once---through a cost-push
($\TFP$) \emph{or} an investment incentive (an ITC), not both. The capital-share lever
($\gammam$) is \emph{not} a third interchangeable view: in the Cobb--Douglas core it
does not crowd capital in at all but instead lowers the energy price and sheds capital,
a distinct factor-income object (\Cref{sec:ch-capint,sec:discussion}). Conflating it
with the cost-of-capital representations is itself a double-counting error.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Revenue must be routed]
A $\tauc$ wedge mechanically generates consumption-tax revenue. That revenue must be
recycled (as transfers or public investment) or explicitly financed (as deficit), or
the run silently embeds an unmodelled fiscal expansion alongside the price shock.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Public and private capital are distinct]
Public-grid capital (reaching \OGCore{} through the public-investment share $\alphaI$
and the public-capital stock $\Kg$) and private-generation capital (reaching the
firm's own capital) touch different objects. Coupling both is complementary, not double
counting.
\end{principle}
