\section{Resource Decisions and Economic Consequences}
\label{sec:intro}

A resource plan can be technically feasible and still have very different consequences
for households, industries and public finances. Lower operating costs may require more
investment today. Cleaner production can improve health. Higher electricity costs can
reduce purchasing power and industrial activity, changing the demand the plan was
designed to meet.

OG--CLEWS brings these consequences into the assessment. CLEWS identifies combinations
of technologies, infrastructure and resource use that satisfy physical and policy
constraints. OG-Core represents the economy's response through production, consumption,
saving, investment and public finance. Households differ by age and lifetime income,
so costs and benefits can be followed through time and across generations.

In the two-way design, resource choices change economic conditions, and the economic
response informs the next resource plan. Repeating the exchange allows demand and
investment choices to adjust together.

OG--CLEWS is being developed within MUIOGO. The eight channels described here focus on
electricity, investment and air pollution. CLEWS also represents land and water, for
which further economic relationships are needed.
