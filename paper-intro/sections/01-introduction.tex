\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

CLEWS identifies feasible combinations of technologies, infrastructure, and resource use
under physical and policy constraints. It can show how an electricity strategy changes
generation, investment, cost, and emissions. It does not show how those changes affect
household budgets, industrial production, wages, saving, public debt, or different
generations.

OG-Core represents those economic relationships. Households differ by age and lifetime
income; industries use labour and capital; government taxes, spends, borrows, and makes
transfers. But OG-Core does not choose power plants or operate an electricity system. An
electricity reform must therefore be translated into the prices, investments, health
effects, and policy instruments that households, firms, and government actually face.

The OG--CLEWS link performs that translation. A solved CLEWS scenario supplies four
changes to OG-Core: electricity cost, public grid investment, the capital intensity of
generation, and particulate emissions. Carbon pricing and private investment incentives
can also be applied as policy inputs. After OG-Core solves, it supplies an equilibrium
return and changes in economic activity that can inform CLEWS's discount rate and demand
path.

Each relationship represents a different economic mechanism. Electricity cost affects
household purchasing power and industrial costs. Grid investment creates public capital
and must be financed. A more capital-intensive generation mix changes the electricity
industry's production structure, while an investment incentive changes its cost of
capital. Lower pollution changes survival and effective labour. Carbon payments create
revenue only where the policy actually collects it. Keeping these effects separate makes
the results interpretable and guards against double counting.

The current system completes the forward pass from CLEWS to OG-Core and emits the two
return paths. A controlled demand change has also passed through a new CLEWS solve. The
system does not yet repeat both models to a mutually consistent solution.
